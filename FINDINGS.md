## Why We Age

Aging is not one thing. It's at least 12 interconnected molecular failures (the "Hallmarks of Aging," López-Otín 2013/2023):

1. DNA accumulates damage faster than it's repaired
2. Telomeres shorten until cells stop dividing
3. The epigenome — the software controlling which genes are on/off — drifts and corrupts
4. Protein quality control fails; misfolded proteins accumulate
5. Cellular recycling (autophagy) slows down
6. Nutrient-sensing pathways (mTOR, insulin/IGF-1) get stuck in "growth mode" when they should shift to "maintenance mode"
7. Mitochondria decline; energy production drops
8. "Zombie" senescent cells accumulate and poison neighbors
9. Stem cells exhaust; regenerative capacity drops
10. Cell-to-cell communication breaks down; chronic inflammation rises
11. Persistent low-grade inflammation ("inflammaging") becomes self-sustaining
12. Gut microbiome deteriorates

These are not independent — they form feedback loops. Upstream failures (DNA damage, epigenetic drift) cascade into downstream ones (senescence, inflammation, stem cell exhaustion).

## Human Health, Function, and Biomarker Findings

These entries address different outcomes and include different study designs. Each claim needs to be read in its original population and endpoint; the table does not establish a uniform effect on biological aging. The headline comparisons and legacy strength labels are prioritized for reappraisal.

| Intervention | What the Data Shows | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Aerobic exercise (Zone 2 + VO2 max intervals)** | VO2 max is the strongest predictor of all-cause mortality. HIIT reversed age-related mitochondrial decline by up to 69%. Nothing else comes close. | Strongest |
| **Resistance training** | Preserves muscle mass, bone density, metabolic health. Reduces fraction of muscle fibers with mtDNA deletions. | Strongest |
| **Sleep (7-9 hrs, consistent)** | Mechanistically linked to glymphatic clearance, hormonal regulation, immune function. | Strong |
| **Caloric restriction (~12% reduction)** | CALERIE RCT (n=218, 2 years): slowed pace of aging by DunedinPACE. Improved cardiometabolic markers, immune function. **Downsides: bone density loss, muscle loss.** | Strong |
| **Not smoking** | Self-evident. | Strong |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** (empagliflozin, etc.) | 25-35% reductions in cardiovascular death in heart failure trials. Likely CR-mimetic effects beyond glucose control. | Strong (in indicated patients) |

## What's Promising but Unproven in Humans

Strong animal data exists. Human trials are either early, small, or have missed primary endpoints.

| Intervention | Mouse Data | Human Reality | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rapamycin** | ITP gold standard: +23-26% lifespan, both sexes | PEARL primary endpoint null. In RAPA-EX-01 (n=40, age 65-85), weekly 6 mg sirolimus did not improve exercise gains and produced more adverse events; sensitivity analyses favored placebo. | Human benefit unproven; dose/schedule safety remains unresolved |
| **Rapamycin + Acarbose (combination)** | ITP: +34% male / +28% female — largest extension ever seen in ITP | No human combination trial. Acarbose alone: +22% male, +5% female (ITP). FDA-approved drug with 20yr safety data. | Most underappreciated ITP hit; no human trial |
| **Canagliflozin (SGLT2 inhibitor)** | ITP: +14% male lifespan extension | CVOTs (EMPA-REG, CANVAS, CREDENCE): cardiovascular mortality reduction in T2D. Mechanism beyond glucose control. | Aging-specific trial not yet designed |
| **Senolytics (D+Q)** | Baker 2011: clearing senescent cells extends lifespan 25-35% | No primary endpoint met in humans. D+Q reaches brain (Phase 1 AD, 2025). CNS demyelination concern (PNAS 2026). | Phase 2 cognitive trial designing now |
| **IL-11 inhibition** | Widjaja 2024 *Nature*: anti-IL-11 extended lifespan ~25% | LASN01 Phase 1 safety complete; no aging trial yet. CALICO $596M deal. Most exciting preclinical target. | Aging trial 2027-2029 est. |
| **Canakinumab (anti-IL-1β)** | N/A (direct human) | CANTOS: 15% MACE reduction + 67% lung cancer incidence reduction in post-MI patients. Fatal infection trade-off. | Healthy aging trial not run; proven in disease |
| **Urolithin A (mitophagy)** | ~19% mouse lifespan extension (unpublished/preclinical) | ATLAS trial: muscle endurance benefit. MitoImmune 2025: naive T cell expansion. CLARITY cognitive trial pending. | Phase 3 needed; mechanism strong |
| **Partial epigenetic reprogramming (OSK)** | Multiple labs: age reversal in mice. Altos Labs publishing. | ER-100 is recruiting 18 people in a first-in-human ocular safety study. No safety, efficacy, or systemic-aging result exists. | First clinical test, not evidence of rejuvenation |
| **Lomecel-B (MSC stem cell therapy)** | N/A | One Phase 2b frailty RCT: +63.4 m in 6-minute walk distance at month 9 in the highest-dose group; more participants improved to Clinical Frailty Scale grade 2–3 (30.8% vs. 14.8%). | Promising, company-funded result; independent replication and Phase 3 needed |
| **Fasting-mimicking diet (FMD)** | Mice: ~10-15% lifespan extension | Phase 2: IGF-1 -15%, insulin -30%, biomarker-based age reduction. No mortality RCT. COI: Longo/L-Nutra. | Independent replication with hard endpoints needed |

## Negative, Mixed, and Insufficient Evidence

These entries include null trials, challenged mechanisms, and inadequate evidence. Those are different conclusions: a study that misses an endpoint does not by itself establish ineffectiveness for every dose, population, or outcome.

| Intervention | What Went Wrong |
|---|---|
| **NMN/NR supplements** | Two 2024 meta-analyses (342 + 513 adults): **zero functional benefits** vs. placebo. NAD+ goes up; nothing measurable improves. "Exaggeration of benefits may exist in the field." (PMIDs: 40275690, 39116016) |
| **Semaglutide in symptomatic Alzheimer's (EVOKE)** | Phase 3 trials (n~3,800): **primary cognitive/functional endpoint not met** (published May 2026). Does not slow clinical decline in established AD. PMID: 41865758. |
| **Taurine (human biomarker hypothesis)** | 2023 Singh *Science* paper claimed taurine declines with age in humans. **This was not confirmed in 2025**: Marcangeli et al. (*Aging Cell*, PMID: 40472098) found no age-related decline in 137 men aged 20–93. Mouse lifespan data stands (10–12%), human mechanism is uncertain. |
| **Metformin (for non-diabetics)** | ITP mouse data negative. RCT meta-analysis (13 trials): no mortality benefit. In METFORAGING (n=40, 96 weeks), the prespecified PhenoAge endpoint was null. Blunts exercise adaptations (multiple RCTs). **TAME has not launched**; the sponsor is still raising funds. |
| **GDF11** | Initially hyped as circulating rejuvenation factor. 2023: actually **exacerbates cardiac injury**. Field "at an impasse." Largely debunked. |
| **Resveratrol (as direct SIRT1 activator)** | Pfizer showed original assay was an artifact. GSK/Sirtris clinical program failed and was abandoned. Poor bioavailability (~1%). May have indirect benefits via AMPK but the original claim is dead. |
| **Fisetin** | Single unreplicated mouse lifespan study. Never tested by NIA ITP. Only human study (n=10) was negative. FIESTA trial results unpublished after years. |
| **TRIIM trial (GH+DHEA+metformin)** | n=9, no controls, not blinded, not replicated. TRIIM-X results never published. Using growth hormone to fight aging contradicts the strong evidence that *reduced* GH/IGF-1 extends lifespan. |
| **TA-65 / telomerase activators** | The best RCT shows ~25 base pairs/year difference — against a background of 5,000–15,000 bp total. No lifespan data. Mendelian randomization shows longer telomeres raise cancer risk (PMID: 28604675). High commercial COI. |
| **He Shou Wu (Fo-Ti)** | Widely sold as a longevity herb. Well-documented hepatotoxicity; multiple FDA warnings and case reports of drug-induced liver injury. Evidence: **negative**. |
| **HBOT for aging** | n=35, no control group, no independent replication. Claims of telomere lengthening not validated. |

## 2025–2026 Updates: What Changed

> **Verified March 2026 against live PubMed searches. Section added after systematic review of post-August 2025 literature.**

### New Null Result: Semaglutide (GLP-1) Failed in Symptomatic Alzheimer's

The EVOKE and EVOKE+ Phase 3 trials (n~3,800) tested oral semaglutide in people with early symptomatic AD. **Their primary clinical endpoint was not met.** The results were published in *The Lancet* in May 2026 (PMID: 41865758); PMID: 39780249 is the earlier trial-design paper, not the outcome report. The trials do not support semaglutide as a treatment for established symptomatic AD.

This is a pattern the field has seen repeatedly: drugs that modify pathology in established disease do not rescue already-damaged circuits. The more promising hypothesis: GLP-1RAs as *prevention* in preclinical aging, not treatment of manifest neurodegeneration.

**What GLP-1RAs do have**: In SELECT (Phase 3, n=17,604), semaglutide reduced the prespecified composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke by 20% in people with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity but no diabetes (HR 0.80; PMID: 37952131). This is strong secondary-prevention evidence, not evidence of a 20% reduction in cardiovascular mortality alone or of general longevity benefit. A randomized trial also reported post-hoc epigenetic-clock changes in people with HIV-associated lipohypertrophy (PMID: 40791720).

### New Druggable Target: GPLD1/TNAP Axis (Exercise → Brain)

Bieri et al. 2026 (*Cell*, PMID: 41713415) resolved how exercise protects the aging brain with molecular precision: exercise stimulates the liver to secrete **GPLD1**, which cleaves **TNAP** from brain blood vessel walls. TNAP accumulates with age and makes the blood-brain barrier leaky. Removing it restores BBB integrity, reduces brain inflammation, and reverses cognitive aging in mice — including in Alzheimer's models.

**Why this matters**: TNAP inhibitors already exist (developed for vascular calcification). This moves "exercise mimetic for the brain" from vague concept to a specific, drugable molecular target. No human trials yet; this is 2026 preclinical data. But it's one of the clearest mechanism-to-drug pipelines in the exercise biology field.

### Taurine: Animal Data Holds, Human Biomarker Hypothesis Challenged

The 2023 Singh *Science* paper (PMID: 37289866) showed taurine extends mouse lifespan 10–12% and proposed that declining taurine with age drives aging. That animal data stands. But the foundational human claim — that taurine *declines* with age — has been directly challenged by a 2025 study of 137 men aged 20–93 (Marcangeli et al., *Aging Cell*, PMID: 40472098) and NIH follow-up work. No age-related decline was found. No association with muscle function, strength, or mitochondrial performance.

**Verdict**: Animal lifespan data is MODERATE evidence. Human relevance is now LOW. Widely consumed globally since 2023 based on extrapolation that has not held up.

### Rapamycin in Older Adults: Exercise Trial Does Not Support Off-Label Use

RAPA-EX-01 (Stanfield et al., 2026, *Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle*, PMID: 41985884) randomized 40 sedentary adults aged 65–85 to weekly sirolimus 6 mg or placebo while both groups completed the same 13-week home exercise program. The intention-to-treat primary endpoint (30-second chair-stands) did not differ significantly (adjusted difference -2.13 repetitions, 95% CI -4.61 to 0.34; p=0.089). Prespecified complete-case and per-protocol analyses significantly favored placebo. The sirolimus arm also had more total adverse events (99 vs. 63), including one possibly drug-related pneumonia.

This was a small, short, exploratory study—not a lifespan trial—and it cannot rule out a different dose or schedule. But it is the most directly relevant published test of the claim that intermittent rapamycin can complement exercise in older people, and it provides no support for that claim. It also means PEARL's tolerability result should not be generalized into a broad “weekly rapamycin is safe” conclusion.

### TAME Is Still a Proposed Trial, Not an Enrolling One

On July 13, 2026, the TAME sponsor, the American Federation for Aging Research, described its six-year trial as occurring **“once launched”** and listed “raising funds to launch” as the current need. There is no published TAME result or enrollment cohort. The trial's composite-endpoint design remains an important regulatory proposal, but it has not established an FDA aging indication. The record previously cited in this repository, NCT03435640, is an unrelated terminated oncology trial, not TAME.

### Metformin Did Not Improve Its Prespecified Epigenetic-Age Endpoint in a 96-Week Pilot

METFORAGING (PMID: 42023167) randomized 40 virologically suppressed, non-diabetic adults aged 50+ living with HIV to metformin 850 mg twice daily or placebo for 96 weeks. Its prespecified primary outcome—between-group change in PhenoAge acceleration in the per-protocol population—was null: -1.02 years (95% CI -5.30 to 3.26; p=0.627). The study was a small, single-centre pilot and closed below its 60-person target, so it cannot settle metformin in the general population. It does, however, add a long-duration randomized null result for a biological-age-clock claim rather than supporting one.

### AI Medtech: Real Disease-Level Signals, Still No Aging Proof

The clearest non-hype milestone is Insilico Medicine's AI-discovered TNIK inhibitor, rentosertib. In a peer-reviewed Phase 2a trial in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (n=71, 12 weeks), the safety primary endpoint was similar to placebo and the highest-dose group had a promising secondary lung-function signal (+98.4 mL forced vital capacity vs. -20.3 mL placebo; PMID: 40461817). That is a real early clinical result—but it is not Phase 3 evidence, not approval, and not a human anti-aging result.

The counterexample matters equally: BioAge Labs, which uses AI/longitudinal biobank data to find metabolic-aging targets, stopped its Phase 2 azelaprag obesity trial after liver transaminitis and terminated the program. AI may improve the starting hypothesis; it does not bypass toxicity or prove a target works.

AI diagnostics are farther along operationally than AI longevity drugs, but implementation is its own test. In a 357-person prospective study, an AI-enabled stethoscope detected moderate-to-severe valvular disease with higher sensitivity than routine auscultation (92.3% vs. 46.2%), finding 12 previously undiagnosed cases (PMID: 41660663). Yet the pragmatic TRICORDER cluster RCT across 205 UK primary-care practices did not increase its population-level heart-failure diagnosis endpoint after 12 months (IRR 0.94, 95% CI 0.86–1.02; PMID: 41619752). Better detection in a study does not automatically become better health outcomes in routine care.

**Verdict:** Treat AI as a research and clinical-workflow tool—not an anti-aging intervention. Demand prespecified clinical endpoints, adequate follow-up, independent replication, real-world implementation evidence, and safety data. AI-derived clocks, target lists, and molecular designs are not evidence that aging has been slowed.

### NMN/NR — Null Results Confirmed by Meta-Analyses

Two 2024 meta-analyses now confirm the null pattern:
- **PMID 40275690**: No effect on muscle mass, grip strength, gait speed, or physical performance in adults >60
- **PMID 39116016**: NAD+ goes up, but: *"Exaggeration of the benefits of NMN supplementation may exist in the field."* Functional outcomes did not differ from placebo

The more promising path is **CD38 inhibition** — blocking the enzyme that degrades NAD+ with age — which extends lifespan ~10% in mice and is mechanistically more targeted than flooding a broken system with precursors.

## What These Findings Can Establish

For each intervention, distinguish a change in mechanism or biomarker, a benefit on a particular clinical outcome, and an effect on human aging or lifespan. The first two do not automatically establish the third.

The purpose of this repository is to identify which claims are supported, which remain uncertain, and which new results would change those judgments. Strong practical health evidence belongs in the supporting guides; promising early research belongs in the research record with its limits visible.

Before relying on a headline, check the original source, exact endpoint, population, uncertainty, harms, and verification date. The [research inbox](./RESEARCH_INBOX.md) tracks the next review priorities.
