# Evidence-Based Personal Care Routine

**Last verified: August 23, 2026 | Scope: recurring skin, hair/scalp, body, nail, foot, and oral care**

This is an operating routine, organized by cadence. It prioritizes interventions with meaningful human evidence, a clear maintenance purpose, and enough exposure time to plausibly work. It is not a review of a product list and does not add weak products merely to make the routine feel comprehensive.

Prescription labels and clinician instructions override this document. Diagnosed-condition treatment is deliberately kept outside the universal routine.

## Evidence and Inclusion Standard

- **HIGH** — replicated controlled human evidence, a strong systematic review, or established guideline-backed care with meaningful clinical outcomes.
- **MODERATE** — positive human evidence with limited duration, indirect comparisons, smaller effects, or some uncertainty.
- **LOW** — small, short, heterogeneous, product-funded, or formulation-transfer evidence.
- **SUPPORT** — useful hygiene, tolerability, or cosmetic maintenance without a strong disease-outcome claim.

The default schedule contains HIGH/MODERATE interventions and low-burden SUPPORT practices. LOW-evidence and diagnosed-condition interventions are not promoted into the routine merely because they are available or prescribed to a particular person.

## Highest-Efficacy Components

| Intervention | Primary outcome | Evidence | Place in routine |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen | Prevent photoaging and UV injury | HIGH | Every morning on ordinarily exposed skin |
| Fluoride toothpaste | Prevent dental caries | HIGH | Twice daily |
| Tolerated moisturizer | Control dryness and support the skin barrier | MODERATE | After cleansing/bathing when needed; around tretinoin if used |
| Daily interdental cleaning | Control interproximal plaque/gingival inflammation | MODERATE as a care standard; LOW for claims that one device is best | Once daily with the tool fitted to the person's anatomy |
| Gentle cleansing, conditioning, foot/nail care | Maintain comfort, cleanliness, and hair/skin integrity | SUPPORT | Daily or weekly according to need |
| Prescription topical tretinoin | Improve facial photodamage and wrinkles | HIGH for the elective goal | Optional evening module at the prescribed tolerated frequency |

## Daily Routine

### Morning

1. **Brush for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste.** Use a soft-bristled manual or powered brush. Standard fluoride toothpaste is the important intervention; expensive toothpaste is not inherently better. The ADA recommends twice-daily fluoride brushing for at least two minutes ([ADA home-care recommendations](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/home-oral-care/)).
2. **Cleanse the face only as needed.** Water alone is acceptable when the face is not oily or carrying residue. When cleanser is useful, choose a gentle, non-abrasive product and avoid scrubs or cleansing brushes ([AAD face-washing guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/face-washing-101)).
3. **Moisturize if the skin needs it.** Apply a tolerated cream to areas that feel dry, tight, flaky, or irritated. Ceramides can be useful, but petrolatum, glycerin, dimethicone, and other ordinary barrier ingredients can also work. No brand has proven universal superiority ([meta-analysis, PMID 37151263](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37151263/)).
4. **Apply broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen.** Cover the face, ears, neck, thinning hairline/scalp, hands, and other skin that is normally exposed. Use enough: the AAD suggests about one teaspoon for face coverage and roughly one ounce for all exposed adult body skin. Use SPF 30+ lip balm when lips are exposed ([AAD application guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/shade-clothing-sunscreen/how-to-apply-sunscreen)).

Daily sunscreen has direct randomized photoaging evidence. In one 4.5-year trial of 903 adults, daily use produced 24% lower odds of an increased photoaging score than discretionary use; that is not the same as 24% fewer wrinkles ([PMID 23732711](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23732711/)). The best sunscreen is a legitimate broad-spectrum product that can be applied generously and consistently.

### During the Day

- **Wash hands with plain soap at ordinary key moments:** after toilet use, before preparing/eating food, after respiratory secretions, after caring for illness, and when visibly dirty. Consumer antibacterial soap has not shown an everyday advantage over plain soap ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/faq/index.html), [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/skip-antibacterial-soap-use-plain-soap-and-water)). Apply hand cream if repeated washing causes roughness or cracking.
- **Reapply sunscreen when exposure requires it.** Reapply about every two hours during sustained outdoor exposure and after swimming or heavy sweating. Ordinary indoor time does not require a timer-driven two-hour reapplication ritual.
- **Avoid repeated high-tension or high-heat hair styling.** Hair-shaft protection is cumulative: lower temperatures, less traction, and gentler handling of wet hair reduce avoidable breakage and traction-related loss.

### Evening

1. **Clean between the teeth once daily.** Use a water flosser, sized interdental brush, floss, or the method recommended for the actual spacing and periodontal anatomy. Short trials favor water jets and interdental brushes for some gingival outcomes, but broader evidence is low certainty and does not prove one device universally prevents periodontitis or tooth loss ([PMID 29520910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29520910/), [Cochrane CD012018](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012018_home-use-devices-cleaning-between-teeth-addition-toothbrushing-prevent-and-control-gum-diseases-and)). Consistent safe use matters more than winning a device comparison.
2. **Brush for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste.** Spit out excess toothpaste. Avoiding a large immediate water rinse retains more fluoride, although the long-term clinical effect size is uncertain ([PMID 35241051](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35241051/)).
3. **Cleanse the face.** Remove sunscreen and normal daily residue with a tolerated gentle cleanser. Double cleansing is unnecessary unless one cleanser fails to remove the product comfortably.
4. **If using the elective tretinoin module, follow the prescribed schedule.** Apply the prescription-directed amount to dry skin if dryness improves tolerability; avoid eyelids, lips, and other irritation-prone areas unless the prescriber says otherwise. A 2025 meta-analysis of eight randomized trials and 1,361 participants found significant improvement in fine and coarse photodamage wrinkles versus vehicle ([PMID 41236273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41236273/)).
5. **Moisturize where needed.** If using tretinoin, apply moisturizer after it, or before and after it when buffering is needed; use moisturizer alone on recovery nights.

#### Tretinoin frequency

- Begin at the frequency set by the prescriber; two nonconsecutive nights per week is a common tolerability ramp.
- Hold a frequency until burning, peeling, and persistent redness are mild or absent.
- Increase by no more than one night per week at a time.
- Do not force nightly use through ongoing irritation. A tolerated lower frequency is more useful than repeated stop-start cycles.
- Do not schedule exfoliating acids, scrubs, or another retinoid during adaptation.

## Bathing and Body Routine

Use this block whenever bathing occurs; there is no evidence-based requirement that every healthy adult shower on the same schedule.

1. **Use warm rather than very hot water when dryness is a concern.** Keep showers reasonably short—about 5–10 minutes is a practical dermatology recommendation for dry skin.
2. **Clean what needs cleaning.** Prioritize underarms, groin/external genital skin, feet, visibly dirty areas, sweat, and sunscreen residue. A gentle wash is preferable to aggressive full-body scrubbing. Comparative trials are insufficient to establish one ideal cleanser or bathing schedule ([AAD dry-skin guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin), [PMID 33724709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33724709/)).
3. **Wash and dry the feet carefully.** Clean between the toes and dry the spaces thoroughly. Put on clean dry socks. This is guideline-based fungal-infection prevention rather than strong randomized evidence ([AAD athlete's-foot guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/athletes-foot-prevent)).
4. **Moisturize dry body skin.** Apply a tolerated cream or ointment after bathing or whenever dryness appears. Damp-skin application is convenient, but a controlled study did not find it superior to delayed application on healthy skin ([PMID 35435321](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35435321/)). Do not apply heavy moisturizer between the toes.
5. **Use antiperspirant or deodorant only for its intended quality-of-life outcome.** It can control sweat or odor but is not a health or anti-aging treatment.
6. **Clean only the outer ears.** Wash the visible outer ear normally and leave the canal alone. Do not insert cotton swabs, camera scoops, or other objects, and do not use ear candles. Asymptomatic wax generally needs no treatment; pain, drainage, bleeding, fullness, or hearing change needs assessment ([AAO-HNS guideline, PMID 28045591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28045591/)).

Fragrance, luxurious texture, and sensory experience are valid preferences for body wash and moisturizer. They do not raise a rinse-off product into a higher evidence tier.

## Hair-Wash Routine

There is no universal number of shampoo days. Wash based on scalp oil, sweat, product buildup, dandruff, hair texture, and styling needs. The AAD notes that this can range from daily for an oily straight-hair scalp to much less often for dry, thick, curly, or tightly coiled hair ([AAD healthy-hair guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/healthy-hair-tips)).

On wash days:

1. Apply shampoo mainly to the scalp rather than repeatedly scrubbing the full hair length.
2. Rinse thoroughly.
3. Use conditioner after shampooing. Apply mostly to the lengths/ends for fine or straight hair and more broadly when the hair is dry or curly.
4. Detangle gently. Wet hair is easier to damage; use fingers or a wide-tooth comb and avoid forceful brushing.
5. Use the lowest heat that achieves the styling goal and avoid styles that repeatedly pull at the hairline.

Use an antidandruff active—such as ketoconazole, selenium sulfide, zinc pyrithione where available, salicylic acid, or piroctone olamine—only when dandruff, itch, or flaking is an actual target. Piroctone evidence for hair retention is limited and industry-associated ([PMID 34424549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34424549/)); shampoo should not be treated as a general hair-growth intervention.

## Shaving and Beard Routine

Use this block whenever shaving; beard grooming without shaving can stop after the cleansing/moisturizing steps.

1. Wash the beard and skin beneath it with a gentle facial cleanser and rinse thoroughly.
2. Soften hair with warm water or shave near the end of a shower.
3. Apply a lubricating shaving cream or gel; avoid dry shaving when irritation or ingrown hairs are a concern.
4. Shave with light pressure in the direction of hair growth. Rinse the razor between passes and avoid repeatedly scraping the same area.
5. Rinse, pat dry, and apply a tolerated fragrance-free moisturizer if the skin feels dry or irritated.
6. Let the razor dry outside the shower. Replace a disposable blade after roughly 5–7 shaves or sooner when dull, and clean an electric razor according to its manual ([AAD shaving guidance](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/hair/how-to-shave)).

Beard conditioner, squalane, or beard oil can improve feel and appearance but is optional cosmetic support. Use a non-comedogenic product if the beard area is acne-prone. Persistent razor bumps, scarring, pustules, or painful ingrown hairs need a different shaving strategy or dermatologic treatment rather than more passes with the razor.

## Weekly or As-Needed Maintenance

- **Nails:** keep fingernails and toenails reasonably short and clean. Clean grooming tools before use, clip rather than bite hangnails, and leave cuticles intact because they form a barrier against infection ([CDC nail hygiene](https://www.cdc.gov/hygiene/about/nail-hygiene.html)).
- **Skin-barrier review:** if dryness or irritation is increasing, first reduce hot water, over-cleansing, scrubbing, fragrance, and overlapping actives. Add products only after identifying an unmet need.
- **Tretinoin recovery:** insert one or more moisturizer-only nights whenever irritation requires it.
- **Hair/scalp:** adjust the wash interval or conditioner placement if oil, flaking, brittleness, or buildup changes.
- **Feet:** apply petrolatum or a 10–25% urea product to dry heels when needed. Cracked, painful, red, draining, or nonhealing skin needs assessment, especially with diabetes or poor circulation.

## Monthly and Quarterly Maintenance

### Monthly

- Take one standardized face photo only if tracking cosmetic treatment. Use the same camera, lighting, distance, angle, and time of day.
- Inspect product tolerability and actual use. A theoretically superior product that is skipped, under-applied, or irritating is not superior in practice.
- Check sunscreen for expiration, separation, odor, color, or texture change. Replace expired or visibly degraded sunscreen ([AAD sunscreen guidance](https://www.aad.org/media/stats-sunscreen)).

### Every three to four months

- Replace the toothbrush or powered-brush head sooner if the bristles are visibly frayed or matted ([ADA toothbrush guidance](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/toothbrushes)). Rinse it after use and let it air dry; routine sanitizer gadgets are unnecessary.

## Clinician-Set Cadence

- **Tretinoin:** the prescriber controls formulation, dose, and frequency, particularly when irritation, eczema, rosacea, pregnancy potential, or other skin treatment changes the risk.
- **Dental examinations and fluoride escalation:** recall interval, fluoride rinse, and prescription-strength toothpaste should follow caries and periodontal risk rather than a universal calendar.

Hair-loss medication, periodontal therapy, acne/dermatitis treatment, antifungals, and other diagnosed-condition care should follow the relevant clinician's plan. They are intentionally not assigned a place in this general routine.

## Items Not Assigned a Recurring Cadence

These categories may have a narrow or personally visible benefit, but current evidence does not justify putting them into the default schedule:

| Category | Evidence | Routine decision |
|---|---:|---|
| Antioxidant serums including topical vitamin C | LOW | No default slot; a 2023 review found only 7 eligible publications and 139 volunteers ([PMID 37128827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37128827/)) |
| PDRN or other “regenerative” cosmetic serums | LOW | No default slot; product-specific penetration and durable outcomes remain unproven ([PMID 42430369](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42430369/)) |
| Home facial delivery/RF/multimode devices | INSUFFICIENT to LOW | No default slot without an independent trial of the exact device and mode |
| Mouthrinse | Risk-dependent | Not universal; consider antimicrobial or fluoride rinse only for a dentist-identified indication |
| Tongue scraper | LOW | Optional for an observed breath/coating benefit, not disease prevention |
| Xylitol gum | LOW/inconsistent | Sugar-free gum may stimulate saliva; a xylitol-specific caries benefit remains uncertain ([PMID 39544205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39544205/)) |

Do not add routine exfoliating acids, peptide/growth-factor serums, separate hyaluronic-acid or niacinamide serums, eye cream, collagen or “hair/skin/nails” supplements, antibacterial soap, blue-light glasses, or another facial device without a specific unmet target and evidence that matches the actual product.

## Minimal Buy and Replacement Policy

The routine requires categories, not luxury brands:

1. Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ face/body sunscreen that can be used generously.
2. SPF 30+ lip balm for ordinarily exposed lips.
3. Gentle facial/body cleanser that does not provoke dryness or irritation.
4. Tolerated face/body moisturizer; cream or ointment where dryness is greater.
5. Soft manual or effective powered toothbrush, fluoride toothpaste, and the interdental tool chosen for the actual anatomy.
6. Shampoo and conditioner selected for scalp and hair type rather than “growth” marketing.
7. Clean nail clippers/file.

Rebuy a premium cleanser, moisturizer, shampoo, conditioner, or sunscreen when its texture, scent, cosmetic finish, or tolerability materially improves consistent use. Do not claim that preference proves superior biological efficacy.

## Routine at a Glance

| Cadence | Actions |
|---|---|
| Every morning | Fluoride brush; cleanse if needed; moisturize if needed; sunscreen |
| Every evening | Interdental clean; fluoride brush; cleanse face; elective tretinoin on prescribed nights; moisturize |
| Each bath/shower | Gentle targeted cleansing; wash/dry feet; moisturize dry skin |
| Each hair wash | Shampoo scalp; condition appropriately; gentle detangling and heat/traction control |
| Each shave | Soften hair; use lubricant; shave with growth; rinse and dry razor; moisturize if needed |
| Weekly/as needed | Nail care; heel care if dry; adjust cleansing/moisture for irritation; recovery night |
| Monthly | Standardized face photo if useful; check sunscreen condition and optional-product value |
| Every 3–4 months | Replace toothbrush/brush head, sooner if frayed |
| Clinician-set | Risk-based dental care; prescription review; other diagnosed-condition care |
