# Preventing Aging

A living research notebook about **whether, how, and how safely we can delay or reverse biological aging and extend healthy life**.

The project follows promising science and tests the claims made about it. It separates discoveries worth investigating from interventions justified for human use. Neither enthusiasm nor skepticism substitutes for evidence.

## Scope

| Area | What belongs here | What a finding can establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Aging biology** | Mechanisms, lifespan experiments, senescence, reprogramming, nutrient sensing, mitochondrial and immune aging | A result in the species, tissue, and experiment studied; human translation is a separate question |
| **Human healthspan** | Mortality, multimorbidity, frailty, disability, cognition, physical function, and age-related disease outcomes | Benefit for the measured outcome and population; disease treatment alone does not establish slowed biological aging |
| **Measurement and translation** | Aging clocks, surrogate validation, trial design, replication, adverse effects, delivery, and regulation | Whether a claim is measurable, reproducible, clinically meaningful, and feasible to test |
| **Supporting health practice** | Evidence-based prevention, exercise, nutrition, sleep, and care | Practical health benefits; keep claims about changing aging itself separate |

General grooming, appearance, productivity, and performance optimization are supporting material, not the organizing purpose of this repository. New content needs an explicit connection to aging biology, human healthspan, or the interpretation of that evidence.

## How to read the evidence

Assess **the claim**, not an entire molecule or field. Record these separately:

- **Context:** species or human population, intervention, route, comparator, and follow-up.
- **Outcome:** survival, clinical events, function, biomarker, or mechanism; primary versus secondary/exploratory.
- **Result:** effect estimate and uncertainty, including null, mixed, or harmful findings.
- **Confidence:** design, bias, precision, consistency, and relevance to the stated claim.
- **Use and access:** applicability, safety, burden, and regulatory status.

A rigorous negative trial can provide strong evidence. An impressive mouse study can provide strong evidence about mice. Funding and approval status matter, but neither is an automatic verdict on efficacy.

**Migration notice:** older prose and [the evidence tracker](./data/evidence-tracker.csv) use a mixed grading scheme, including HIGH for some animal findings and DEBUNKED for some negative results. Those labels have not been systematically reassessed. Read them with their population and endpoint; do not treat them as a ranking of human anti-aging treatments. New and revised claims follow [the maintenance standard](./EVIDENCE_MAINTENANCE.md).

## Currency

The existing research reports record a March 2026 core review and selected July 2026 updates; supporting guides have their own dates. **The October 30, 2026 scope and methodology cleanup is not a new literature review.** Historical trial status, forecasts, and claims of exhaustive verification need checking before reuse.

Use the research inbox to record a search window, sources, findings, and decisions. A document edit does not refresh every scientific claim in it. The proposed review cadence is documented; no automatic monitoring is configured by these files.

This is a research reference, not an individualized treatment plan.
