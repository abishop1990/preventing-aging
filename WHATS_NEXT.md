# What Could Change the Picture

**Last updated: July 13, 2026 (targeted trial-status update)**

> **Editorial scope update: October 30, 2026; no new trial-status review.** Dates, recruitment descriptions, and projected readouts below are historical and pending verification. A trial completion estimate is not a results-publication date. Use [RESEARCH_INBOX.md](./RESEARCH_INBOX.md) for the current review queue.

This document records research questions and programs that could change the evidence about aging biology and human healthspan. Evaluate each program against its intended endpoint, population, uncertainty, and safety; avoid treating all disease-treatment and aging claims as the same question.

This document asks: **which results would change what we know about aging and its treatment?**

---

## 1. Trials That Could Change Everything

These are programs selected in the prior review for their potential relevance. Their current status and projected timing need verification; the ordering is editorial, not a quantified estimate of success.

### GLP-1 Agonists in Neurodegeneration — Mixed Results, Prevention Hypothesis Survives

**Update (published May 2026):** EVOKE and EVOKE+ Phase 3 trials (oral semaglutide in early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease, n~3,800) **missed their primary clinical endpoint** (*Lancet*, PMID: 41865758). PMID: 39780249 is the study-design paper, not the outcome report. The trials did not show slowed cognitive or functional decline in established symptomatic disease.

**What survives:** SELECT showed a 20% reduction in its composite MACE endpoint (cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, or nonfatal stroke) in people with established cardiovascular disease and overweight/obesity without diabetes—not a 20% reduction in cardiovascular mortality alone. GLP-1RAs reduce inflammation, amyloid burden, and neuroinflammation in preclinical models. The more promising hypothesis — not yet tested — is GLP-1RAs as *prevention* in preclinical aging. Epidemiological dementia associations remain non-randomized evidence. First aging-specific prevention trial timing is uncertain.

- Sabbagh et al. 2026 (*Nature Aging*, PMID: 41419667): comprehensive review calling GLP-1RAs an emerging geroscience modality for *prevention*, not treatment
- **Revised verdict**: Not the first pharmacy-available geroscience drug for neurodegeneration. Still potentially the first for cardiovascular aging. Prevention trial data needed before reassessment.

### TAME Trial — The Regulatory Paradigm Shift

**Status correction (July 13, 2026): TAME has not launched or begun enrollment.** Its official sponsor, the American Federation for Aging Research, says the six-year study will occur “once launched” and identifies “raising funds to launch” as the current need. Its 3,000-participant, 14-site composite-endpoint design remains a proposal; no results timeline is supportable until it launches. The previously cited NCT03435640 is an unrelated terminated oncology trial, not TAME.

TAME's primary value is regulatory as well as scientific. A positive result could support a composite multimorbidity-prevention endpoint, but it would **not by itself** establish “aging” as an FDA-approvable indication or automatically create labels for other geroscience therapies. Any such change would require separate FDA action and evidence for a defined indication.

TAME biomarker infrastructure (23-protein panel) is already being deployed cross-study (Delhaes et al. 2026, PMID: 41655127).

### Dog Aging Project TRIAD — First Mammalian Lifespan Trial Outside a Lab

Design published Feb 2025 (PMID: 39951177). TRIAD is a randomized rapamycin-versus-placebo trial with lifespan as its primary endpoint and a **target enrollment of 580 dogs**. The protocol reported 158 enrolled at the time of writing; no current official completion announcement or efficacy result was located.

Dogs share human living environments and diseases. If rapamycin extends dog lifespan, it would provide unusually relevant translational evidence for a human trial. No results date should be treated as committed until enrollment and follow-up are publicly confirmed.

### IL-11 Inhibitors — Waiting for Fibrosis Data

No human aging trials yet. Eleven Therapeutics (Cook group spinout) and GSK are in **Phase 1 for fibrosis** (IPF, cardiac). The 2024 *Nature* mouse data (22-25% lifespan extension) is extraordinary, but human aging trials are **2-4 years away**. Fibrosis results serve as proof-of-concept for the aging hypothesis.

### Senolytics — CNS Penetration Confirmed, Phase 3 Bottleneck

D+Q Phase 1 Alzheimer's (n=5, PMID: 40274471): **dasatinib confirmed to reach the brain** in humans. Inflammatory gene downregulation observed. Phase 2 with cognitive endpoints in design — results **2027-2028**.

**But no senolytic has entered Phase 3** — bottlenecked on senescent cell biomarker validation. Mayo Clinic found current p16 primer designs cross-detect isoforms, potentially invalidating trial results (Farr et al. 2025, PMID: 39823170). First Phase 3 realistically **2027-2028** pending biomarker standardization.

### Epigenetic Reprogramming — First-in-Human Safety Study Is Recruiting

Life Biosciences' ER-100 is now the first registered human partial-reprogramming study: NCT07290244 is recruiting 18 people with open-angle glaucoma or non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy. The registry lists a March 2, 2026 start and a May 2027 primary-completion estimate. This is an AAV-delivered OSK safety/tolerability study in an eye disease, **not** a systemic aging trial; no human safety or efficacy results have been reported. Altos Labs remains preclinical. Systemic-aging use remains much farther away.

### Combination Geroscience Trials — The Industry is Organizing

**ICFSR Task Force 2025** (PMID: 41863810) — with Pfizer, Hevolution Foundation, Calico, and Rejuvenate Biomed at the table — identified combination multi-hallmark therapy as the highest-priority unmet need. They're building a minimum common dataset standard for all geroscience trials. Combination trials expected to **launch 2026-2028**.

ITP already showed rapamycin + acarbose is superadditive in male mice (PMID: 36179270). No human combination aging trial exists yet. This is the biggest gap.

## 2. Combination Therapy: Targeting Multiple Hallmarks

The strongest signal that we could go beyond "common sense" comes from combinations. Single interventions hit one hallmark; aging involves 12.

**Rapamycin + Acarbose (ITP, PMID: 36179270)**:
- In male mice, the combination produced lifespan benefits **exceeding either drug alone** — superadditive
- This is the first ITP evidence that drug combinations can beat individual drugs
- No human combination trial exists

**Church's 3-gene therapy (Davidsohn/Church et al. 2019, PMID: 31685628)**:
- FGF21 + αKlotho + sTGFβR2 delivered via AAV
- Single formulation treated **4 age-related diseases simultaneously**: heart failure (+58% function), renal failure (-75% atrophy), obesity (complete reversal), type 2 diabetes (complete reversal)
- A multi-hallmark approach in one shot — the most compelling preclinical combination result

**Conboy OT+A5i — why the combination works (PMID: 40536399)**:
- Oxytocin alone has age-diminished effects. Alk5 inhibitor alone causes excessive TGF-β suppression.
- Together: dose reduction enables pathway optimization neither achieves alone
- 73% life extension from treatment start in old male mice (Kato et al. 2025, PMID: 40848270)
- Female mice not responsive after 4 months — sex-specific, needs investigation

**Theoretical framework (Panchin et al. 2024, PMID: 39159129)**:
- "Solving the problem of aging may require targeting not one but many of its underlying causes at once"
- Even if each hallmark intervention is modest alone, combinations could stack

**The gap**: No human trial has tested a multi-hallmark aging combination. This is perhaps the biggest untested hypothesis in the field.

## 3. Exercise Mimetics: Can You Put Exercise in a Pill?

If exercise is the #1 intervention but not everyone can do it (disability, frailty, extreme old age), can we replicate its benefits pharmacologically?

**Current status: No.**

| Candidate | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **MOTS-c** (mitochondrial peptide) | Most promising preclinically. Improved physical performance across all ages in mice. Zero human efficacy trials. | PMID: 33473109 |
| **Irisin** (FNDC5 myokine) | Observational human data links to muscle/cognition. No pharmaceutical formulation. No RCTs for aging. | PMID: 40420047 |
| **GW501516** (PPARδ, "exercise pill") | **Development abandoned** — safety issues in Phase II | PMID: 24428677 |
| **AICAR** (AMPK activator) | No human aging data whatsoever | Preclinical only |
| **Urolithin A** | Most advanced clinically but primary endpoints repeatedly missed | See main report |

**Bottom line**: Exercise mimetics remain 5-10+ years from clinical application. Exercise itself remains irreplaceable. MOTS-c is the one to watch.

**Important 2026 caution on rapamycin + exercise:** RAPA-EX-01 (PMID: 41985884) randomized 40 sedentary adults aged 65–85 to weekly sirolimus 6 mg or placebo during a 13-week home exercise program. Sirolimus did not improve the primary functional endpoint; prespecified sensitivity analyses favored placebo, and total adverse events were higher (99 vs. 63). It was small and exploratory, so it does not settle all doses or schedules, but it does not support the popular claim that pulsed rapamycin augments exercise adaptation.

## 4. New Models: Bypassing the Pharma/FDA Bottleneck

If the regulatory system prevents proper testing, can new trial models go around it?

| Model | Scale | What It's Generating | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dog Aging Project (TRIAD)** | Large longitudinal Dog Aging Project Pack; TRIAD target is 580 dogs | Rapamycin lifespan RCT in companion dogs. Protocol published in *GeroScience*. | Valuable translational model; no efficacy result yet. |
| **AgelessRx PEARL** | ~100 participants | Decentralized RCT for rapamycin. Published. Primary endpoint null. | Proved decentralized trials are feasible. COI issues. |
| **N-of-1 + wearables** | Thousands tracked | CosinorAge from 80,000 wearable users predicts healthspan (PMID: 38834756). Activity profiles stratify mortality risk. | Data exists but fragmented. No coordination. |
| **Epigenetic clock community trials** | Small pilots | Fitzgerald et al. 2021 (PMID: 33844651): first RCT showing epigenetic age reversal from lifestyle. | Rapid readout. Scalable. Most promising format for citizen science. |
| **Blockchain/DAO trials** | Theoretical | Zero implementations in aging. No legal shortcut past FDA/IRB for human subjects. | Not viable yet. |

**Most promising path**: Standardize epigenetic clock testing as a rapid-readout endpoint → run large decentralized trials on cheap interventions (lifestyle, generic drugs, supplements) → generate the population-level data the FDA pathway can't produce at scale.

## 5. Regulatory Reform — What Would Unlock the Field

The single most impactful change would be **FDA biomarker qualification for DunedinPACE or GrimAge2** as a surrogate endpoint. This would allow shorter, cheaper trials using biological age as the outcome instead of waiting for disease/death — cutting trial costs by 10-100x and timelines from decades to years.

**What's happening:**
- **TAME funding remains unresolved:** AFAR's current official status page says it is still raising funds to launch TAME. Therefore, claims that enrollment has already been unblocked should not be relied on.
- **ICFSR Task Force** (PMID: 41863810): Pfizer, Calico, Hevolution building minimum common dataset for geroscience trials
- **Adaptive platform trials** (like RECOVERY for COVID): test multiple aging drugs in one infrastructure — proposed but not yet launched for aging
- **TAME as regulatory wedge**: if positive, establishes aging as an FDA-approvable composite indication
- **No Congressional legislation** specifically targeting aging research reform has been identified in published literature

**What would change everything:**
1. FDA accepts DunedinPACE as surrogate endpoint → trials go from 10 years/3000 people to 2 years/200 people. **Realistic timeline: submission ~2028-2030, qualification ~2030-2033.** The "Holy Grail" (accelerated drug approval based on clock data) is **2033-2040** in the optimistic scenario.
2. A RECOVERY-style platform trial for aging → test rapamycin, senolytics, lamivudine, AKG, spermidine simultaneously. **No design paper exists yet.** The Kritchevsky 2025 Delphi (PMID: 39989956, 28 experts) found no full consensus on composite endpoints — a prerequisite for any platform trial.
3. NIH/NIA budget increase specifically earmarked for generic drug aging trials. **No Congressional legislation specifically targeting aging research reform exists as of March 2026.** NIA budget is under pressure.
4. Patent reform or incentive structure for generic drug repurposing (e.g., extended exclusivity for new indications)

**The strategic choice the field made**: They deliberately did NOT ask FDA to classify aging as a disease. Instead, TAME uses a composite multimorbidity endpoint as a regulatory wedge — if it works, aging becomes treatable by stealth, without FDA ever formally reclassifying it. (Barzilai et al. 2016, PMID: 27304507)

## 6. Emerging Interventions That Could Change Things

These are findings from 2024-2026 that didn't make it into most longevity summaries but have real potential.

### Lamivudine for Aging — The Most Actionable Finding

**This might be the single most important discovery we found.**

LINE-1 retrotransposons (17% of the human genome, normally silenced) reactivate with age. They generate cytoplasmic DNA that triggers the cGAS-STING innate immune pathway → type-I interferon → SASP → inflammaging. Sedivy et al. 2019 (*Nature*, PMID: 30728521) validated this causally in mice.

**Lamivudine** — an FDA-approved, generic, cheap, extensively safety-tested HIV/hepatitis drug (nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor) — blocks LINE-1 reverse transcription, reducing inflammation in aged mice.

A new drug target was also identified: **LINE-1 endonuclease inhibitors** (D'Ordine/Sedivy 2024, *Nature Communications*, PMID: 38719805). Transposon Therapeutics (Sedivy's company) is developing these.

**Why this matters**: Lamivudine is immediately testable. It's generic, safe, cheap, and targets a validated aging mechanism. It faces the same patent problem as rapamycin/metformin — no one profits from proving it works. But it could enter an aging clinical trial *tomorrow*.

### GPLD1/TNAP — Exercise-for-the-Brain, Now Druggable

Bieri et al. 2026 (*Cell*, PMID: 41713415) resolved how exercise benefits the aging brain:

- Liver secretes GPLD1 after exercise → cleaves TNAP from brain vasculature → restores blood-brain barrier transport → rejuvenates cognition
- TNAP increases with age on cerebral vasculature and impairs cognitive function
- Inhibiting TNAP directly recapitulated exercise's brain benefits — including in Alzheimer's mouse models
- **TNAP inhibitors already exist** (developed for vascular calcification)

This moves "exercise mimetic for the brain" from vague concept to specific drug target.

### Plasma Dilution — Already Tested in Humans

The Conboy lab's key insight: parabiosis works not by adding young factors but by **removing old ones**. Neutral blood exchange rejuvenates mice as effectively as young blood.

**Already in humans**: Kim/Conboy 2022 (*Geroscience*, PMID: 35999337) — therapeutic plasma exchange in older humans reduced biological age by a protein biomarker panel. The infrastructure exists (TPE is standard clinical practice for autoimmune conditions). Limitation: requires repetition, transient effect, panel was non-standard (not epigenetic clocks).

### GLP-1 Agonists (Semaglutide) — Aging-Adjacent Data Emerging

Not a longevity drug — but the data keeps expanding beyond metabolic disease:
- SELECT trial: 20% reduction in composite MACE in people with established CVD and overweight/obesity without diabetes
- Extensive preclinical neuro data: reduced neuroinflammation, amyloid, tau, alpha-synuclein (62 studies reviewed, PMID: 40252880)
- **First dedicated aging biomarker trial** registered: semaglutide effects on lean mass, physical function, and epigenetic aging in older adults with insulin resistance (PMID: 39269759)

Already in millions of people. The question is whether aging-specific benefit exists beyond metabolic effects. Answer in 2-3 years.

### Alpha-Ketoglutarate — Enrolled, but Still No Results

AKG is a TCA cycle intermediate that declines ~10-fold between ages 40-80. Cofactor for TET demethylases (epigenetic regulation). The **ABLE trial** (Singapore, NCT05706389; PMID: 37217632) enrolled 120 participants and is now listed as active, not recruiting. Its registry entry lists primary completion in August 2025 and final study completion in January 2027; no results have been posted. Until a results paper or registry results appear, AKG remains unproven despite its availability as a supplement.

### Klotho — Human Genetics Getting Stronger

The KL-VS variant continues accumulating evidence: reduced neuroinflammation (PMID: 39030746), lower amyloid positivity (PMID: 39529379), better brain structure (PMID: 41627838). But klotho protein can't be manufactured at scale for injection, and AAV-klotho gene therapy is preclinical. The PF4 connection (klotho induces PF4 secretion) suggests an indirect druggable path.

### Conboy OT+A5i — 73% Lifespan Extension, But Questions

The most dramatic mouse result of 2025. Old frail males treated with oxytocin + ALK5 inhibitor: 73% extension from treatment start. Both drugs exist as approved medications. But: **only males benefited** (females showed no lifespan gain), single lab, not replicated, ALK5 inhibition carries chronic cancer risk. An erratum was published. Watch but don't act on this yet.

## The Real Question

The gap between "exercise and eat well" and "drugs that reverse aging" won't close by finding a better supplement. It'll close when one of these things happens:

1. **A trial actually works** — TRIAD (rapamycin in dogs) or an IL-11 inhibitor in humans shows unambiguous lifespan/healthspan extension
2. **FDA accepts an aging biomarker** — DunedinPACE gets qualified as a surrogate endpoint, enabling shorter, cheaper trials
3. **Combination therapy is tested** — multi-hallmark approaches (rapamycin + senolytics + reprogramming?) are tested in humans
4. **Someone runs a properly powered trial on a generic drug** — not a 48-week decentralized trial by a tiny startup, but a TAME-scale effort that actually finishes

Until then, the honest answer remains: exercise, sleep, eat plants, don't smoke, don't drink, have friends, find purpose. Not because the drugs can't work — but because we haven't been able to prove they do.
